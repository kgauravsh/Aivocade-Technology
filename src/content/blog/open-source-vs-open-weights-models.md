## 1. Open Source Model (Focus: Code)

When an AI model is described as "open source," it means that its *entire codebase* and underlying software architecture are publicly available under an open-source license (like MIT or Apache).

**What you get:**

- **The Training Method/Architecture:** The detailed recipe, the libraries used, and the instructions on how to build and run the model.
- **Reproducibility:** Developers can inspect the code, modify it, fix vulnerabilities, and understand *how* every part works.

**Analogy:** Open Source is like getting a "full DIY car kit". You get the engine (weights), but more importantly, you also get all the wiring diagrams, blueprints, specialized tools, and assembly instructions (the open-source code) needed to assemble it yourself.

**Key Takeaway:** With open source, you have transparency into the "process" of AI creation.

## 2. Open Weights Model (Focus: Parameters/Knowledge)

When an AI model is described as "open weights," it means that the final, trained parameters - the numerical values learned during the massive training process are made publicly available for anyone to download and use. These weights are the core intellectual property of the trained model.

**What you get:**

- **The Weights (Parameters):** The billions or trillions of numbers that define how the model responds and what knowledge it possesses (e.g., "If the input is X, output Y").
- **Usage Freedom:** You can download these weights and run them on your own hardware for free or at low cost.

**Analogy:** Open Weights is like downloading a "finished blueprint of a complex engine". You get the complete mathematical structure (the numbers), but you might still need proprietary software, drivers, or specific licenses to physically make it work in real-world applications.

**Key Takeaway:** With open weights, you have transparency into the knowledge and output of the AI.
